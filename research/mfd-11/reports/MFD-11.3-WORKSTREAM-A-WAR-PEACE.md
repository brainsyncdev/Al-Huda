## Section 1: Concept Inventory

This investigation examined six concept families within the war-peace-conflict domain. The analysis reveals that these are NOT six independent parallel topics but rather form an interconnected semantic network with distinct lexical roots, overlapping contexts, and hierarchical relationships.

### 1.1 Jihād (j-h-d) — Striving / Exertion

**Definition:** The Qur'anic concept of *jihād* derives from root j-h-d (Form III verb *jāhada*, masdar *jihād*) meaning "to strive, exert effort, struggle." It is a BROAD concept encompassing multiple modalities:

- **Spiritual striving:** Perseverance in faith despite hardship (29:69, 22:78, 3:142, 47:31)
- **Verbal/Qur'anic striving:** Advocacy through revelation, not arms (25:52)
- **Material/personal sacrifice:** Expending wealth and self in Allah's cause (9:41, 9:44, 9:81, 9:86, 9:88, 49:15, 61:11, 8:72, 8:74-75, 2:218, 16:110, 4:95)
- **Prophetic confrontation:** Striving against disbelievers AND hypocrites (includes non-combatants internally; cf. 66:9 noted in MFD-11.2)
- **Martial context:** When paired with *infirū* (go forth) or *qitāl* vocabulary, jihād takes on military mobilization sense (9:41 specifically)

**Boundaries:** Jihād TRANSCENDS the war/peace binary. It is fundamentally about effort/exertion in divine cause. Only a SUBSET of jihād verses relate to armed conflict. The majority address spiritual perseverance, material sacrifice, or intellectual advocacy.

**Relationship to other concepts:** Jihād is the UMBRELLA concept under which qitāl (armed fighting) operates as one modality. However, jihād also encompasses non-martial striving that belongs to different domains (spiritual-development, faith-practice).

**Key morphological note:** All cited verses use Form III verb *jāhada* or its active participle *mujāhidūn* or masdar *jihād*. No Form I *jahada* appears in the war-peace corpus.

### 1.2 Qitāl (q-t-l) — Armed Fighting

**Definition:** Root q-t-l produces Form I *qatala* (to kill), Form III *qātala/yuqātilu* (to fight/combat), and masdar *qitāl* (fighting/warfare). This is the SPECIFIC Qur'anic term for armed combat.

**Sub-concepts established by the text:**
- **Defensive permission:** 22:39-40 — first explicit permission to fight, grounded in being wronged (*ẓulimū*) and expelled from homes
- **Proportionality/restraint:** 2:190 (*lā taʿtadū* — do not transgress), 2:193 (cease when fitnah ends)
- **Obligation despite aversion:** 2:216 (*kutiba ʿalaykum al-qitāl*)
- **Liberation theology:** 4:75 (fight for the oppressed *mustadʿafīn*)
- **Collective solidarity:** 61:4 (fight in ranks like a solid structure)
- **Sacred months exception:** 2:194, 9:36
- **Fitnah cessation as objective:** 2:193, 8:39
- **Prisoner treatment:** 47:4 (*mann* or *fidāʾ*)
- **Booty distribution:** 8:41
- **Deterrence/preparation:** 8:60

**Boundaries:** Qitāl is strictly MARTIAL. It does not encompass spiritual striving, verbal advocacy, or financial sacrifice unless explicitly linked to combat context.

**Relationship to jihād:** Qitāl is a SUBSET of jihād in martial contexts. Verses using q-t-l should NOT be classified under "jihad-definition" unless they also employ j-h-d vocabulary. Current taxonomy conflates these.

### 1.3 Ṣulḥ (ṣ-l-ḥ) — Reconciliation / Settlement

**Definition:** Root ṣ-l-ḥ produces Form I maṣdar *ṣulḥ* (reconciliation, settlement, making peace) and Form IV *aṣlaḥa* (to reconcile, reform, make right between parties).

**Contexts in the corpus:**
- **Marital reconciliation:** 4:35 (*iṣlāḥan* between spouses), 4:128 (*ṣulḥan* — settlement is best)
- **Inter-communal conflict resolution:** 49:9-10 (*fa-aṣliḥū baynahumā* — make settlement between them; believers are brothers)
- **Peacemaking during war:** 8:61 (*in janaḥū li-l-silm fa-jnaḥ lahā* — if they incline to peace/silm, incline to it)
- **General social reconciliation:** 2:224 (*wa-an tuṣliḥū bayna al-nās*), 2:182 (correcting bequest errors)

**Boundaries:** Ṣulḥ is primarily INTERPERSONAL and COMMUNAL reconciliation. In 8:61 it extends to interstate/conflict peacemaking. It is distinct from treaty-making (ʿahd/mīthāq) which is contractual; ṣulḥ is relational/restorative.

**Relationship to salām/silm:** Overlaps significantly. 8:61 uses *silm* (not ṣulḥ) for wartime peacemaking. Ṣulḥ emphasizes the PROCESS of reconciliation; silm/salām emphasizes the STATE of peace.

**Note on 24:26:** This verse does NOT contain ṣ-l-ḥ root. It addresses moral purity (*ṭayyibāt*). Incorrectly listed in task prompt; verified absent from corpus extraction.

### 1.4 ʿAhd / Mīthāq (ʿ-h-d / w-th-q) — Covenant / Binding Commitment

**Definition:** Two distinct but related roots:
- **ʿAhd** (ʿ-h-d): covenant, pledge, treaty, promise. Noun *ʿahd*; verb *ʿāhada* (to covenant/treaty).
- **Mīthāq** (w-th-q): solemn covenant, binding charter, firm agreement. Noun of instrument/time from Form I *wathaqa* (to bind firmly).

**Three tiers of covenant in the Qur'an:**
1. **Divine-human covenants:** Allah's covenant with Bani Israel (2:40, 2:80, 5:12-14), with prophets (3:81 referenced in MFD-11.2), with Abraham (2:124), with believers (5:7, 48:10 bayʿah)
2. **Inter-communal treaties:** Political/military alliances (9:4, 9:7, 8:56-58, 8:72, 4:90 mīthāq)
3. **Individual moral commitments:** Personal oaths and promises (16:91-92, 17:34, 23:8, 33:15, 33:23, 70:32)

**Key structural finding:** The Qur'an treats ALL three tiers with the same vocabulary and moral weight. Breaking ANY covenant incurs divine accountability (3:77, 13:25, 16:92). This means "peace agreements" cannot be cleanly separated from general covenant theology without losing Qur'anic coherence.

**Relationship to peace-agreements:** Peace treaties are ONE APPLICATION of the broader covenant concept. Verses 5:1 (*awfū bi-l-ʿuqūd*) and 16:91 (*awfū bi-ʿahd Allāh*) are general covenant commands that UNDERGIRD treaty obligations but are not exclusively about peace treaties.

### 1.5 Salām / Silm (s-l-m) — Peace / Submission / Safety

**Definition:** Root s-l-m is POLYSEMOUS, producing:
- **Salām** (maṣdar/noun): peace, safety, wholeness; divine greeting (16:32, 19:62, 25:63, 25:75, 33:44, 36:58, 39:73, 56:26, 97:5); divine name (59:23 *al-Salām*)
- **Silm** (noun): peace/submission as a state or condition (2:208 *udkhulū fī l-silm kaffatan*, 8:61 *janaḥū li-l-silm*, 47:35 *tadʿū ilā l-silm*)
- **Islam/Muslim:** Same root; submission to God. Polysemy means every salām/silm reference carries theological resonance.

**Critical polysemy issue:** 2:208 (*udkhulū fī l-silm kaffatan*) is translated as "enter into Islam completely" OR "enter into peace completely." Both readings are lexically valid. Saheeh International renders it "Islam" but the immediate context (following verses about Satan as enemy) supports either reading. This verse CANNOT be unambiguously assigned to either "peace" or "submission" without interpretive commitment.

**Eschatological vs. temporal peace:** Most salām references describe PARADISE greetings or divine attributes, not earthly political peace. Only 8:61, 47:35, and possibly 2:208 address temporal/historical peace.

**Relationship to ṣulḥ:** Salām is the STATE; ṣulḥ is the PROCESS. 8:61 bridges both: inclination toward silm triggers reciprocal response.

### 1.6 Justice / Aggression / Restraint / Protection / Treaty Obligations

This is not a single lexical family but a CROSS-CUTTING ethical framework distributed across multiple roots:

- **Restraint (lā taʿtadū):** 2:190 — foundational proportionality principle
- **Sanctity of life:** 5:32 (killing one soul = killing all humanity), 6:151, 17:31, 17:33
- **Justice even toward enemies:** 5:8 (*lā yajrimannakum shanaʾānu qawmin ʿalā allā taʿdilū*), 60:8
- **Non-hostile kindness:** 60:8-9 (birr and qisṭ toward non-fighters)
- **Asylum:** 9:6 (*istajāra* — grant protection so they hear Allah's words)
- **Feeding captives:** 76:8 (*yuṭʿimūna al-ṭaʿām ʿalā ḥubbihi miskīnan wa-yatīman wa-asīran*)
- **Treaty breach consequences:** 8:56-58 (*nabdh* — throw back treaty on equal terms)
- **Withdrawal immunity:** 4:90 (those who withdraw and offer peace have no case against them)
- **Oppressed protection:** 4:75 (fight for mustadʿafīn)

**Provenance classification (building on MFD-11.1):**
- QURANIC-EXPLICIT: lā taʿtadū (2:190), asylum (9:6), feeding captives (76:8), non-hostile kindness (60:8), sanctity of life (5:32), justice toward enemies (5:8), withdrawal immunity (4:90)
- HADITH-DERIVED: Enumerated non-combatant categories (women, children, monks, trees, orchards) — NONE appear in the Qur'anic text itself. These are Prophetic traditions recorded in hadith collections.

**Relationship to other concepts:** This ethical framework CUTS ACROSS jihād, qitāl, ṣulḥ, and ʿahd. It is not a standalone topic but a set of CONSTRAINTS and OBLIGATIONS that modify all other concepts.

### 3.2 Cross-Cutting Issues

1. **Covenant (ʿahd/mīthāq) has no dedicated topic** despite being the MOST FREQUENTLY occurring concept family in this domain (30+ verses). It currently leaks into peace-agreements (5:1, 16:91) and is otherwise unmapped.

2. **Salām/silm polysemy unresolved.** 2:208 could anchor either a "peace" topic or a "submission/Islam" topic. Eschatological salām (majority of occurrences) belongs to eschatology/theology domains, not war-peace.

3. **Ethical constraints are scattered.** Proportionality, sanctity of life, justice toward enemies, asylum, feeding captives — these form a coherent ETHICAL FRAMEWORK but are split across rules-of-engagement, peace-agreements, and unmapped space.

4. **Phantom related topics persist:** jihad-types, greater-lesser-jihad, treaty-fulfillment, conflict-prevention — all referenced but nonexistent.

### 3.3 Empty Dimension Assessment: conflict-resolution

The `conflict-resolution` dimension is currently empty but the Qur'an establishes a ROBUST conflict-resolution framework:

- **Inter-believer mediation:** 49:9-10 provides a complete protocol: (1) attempt ṣulḥ, (2) if one party oppresses, fight oppressor until compliance, (3) then ṣulḥ with justice, (4) believers are brothers.
- **Marital arbitration:** 4:35 establishes dual-arbitrator mechanism.
- **Social reconciliation:** 2:224 commands making peace among people.
- **Bequest correction:** 2:182 permits corrective reconciliation.

This dimension SHOULD contain at minimum a topic on **ṣulḥ-reconciliation** covering interpersonal, communal, and inter-factional conflict resolution.

## Section 5: Scholarly Questions

1. **Jihād-Qitāl Relationship:** Does the Qur'an present jihād and qitāl as a unified concept with martial and non-martial dimensions, or as two fundamentally distinct concepts that later Islamic tradition merged? The answer determines whether SPLIT (into jihad-striving + qital-fighting) or REDEFINE (expanded jihad encompassing both) is taxonomically correct. Evidence points toward distinction: 25:52 explicitly defines jihād through the Quran (not arms), while 2:190-194 exclusively uses q-t-l for combat legislation.

2. **Covenant Scope Boundary:** Should verses 5:1 (*awfū bi-l-ʿuqūd*) and 16:91 (*awfū bi-ʿahd Allāh*) remain under `peace-agreements`, or should a separate `covenant-ahd-mithaq` topic be created? These verses address ALL contractual/covenantal obligations, not exclusively peace treaties. Their inclusion under peace-agreements conflates the specific with the general. However, creating a separate topic risks fragmenting what the Qur'an presents as a unified ethical principle.

3. **2:208 Silm Interpretation:** Should 2:208 (*udkhulū fī l-silm kaffatan*) be classified under war-peace-conflict (as "enter peace completely") or under belief-theology (as "enter Islam/submission completely")? Both readings are lexically valid. The immediate context (warning against Satan's footsteps) supports either. This verse may need DUAL MAPPING or an explicit interpretive note acknowledging the polysemy.

4. **Conflict-Resolution Dimension Population:** Given that 49:9-10 provides a complete inter-believer conflict resolution protocol (mediation → enforcement → just settlement → brotherhood affirmation), should `conflict-resolution` be populated with a `sulh-reconciliation` topic? Or does this content belong under a broader `social-justice` or `community-governance` domain? The Qur'an treats ṣulḥ as applicable to marital, communal, and interstate contexts simultaneously.

5. **Non-Combatant Protection Redefinition:** Since enumerated non-combatant categories are hadith-derived, should `non-combatant-protection` be (a) REDEFINED to cover Qur'anic restraint principles (la taʿtadū, sanctity of life, non-hostile kindness), (b) MERGED into `rules-of-engagement`, or (c) REMOVED entirely with its Qur'anic content redistributed? Option (a) preserves the important concept while grounding it in actual Qur'anic text.

6. **Eschatological Salām Separation:** The majority of salām references (16:32, 19:62, 25:75, 33:44, 36:58, 39:73, 56:26) describe Paradise greetings or divine attributes, not earthly peace. Should these be excluded from war-peace-conflict entirely and mapped to eschatology/theology topics instead? Only 8:61, 47:35, 4:90, and possibly 2:208 are genuinely within the war-peace domain.

7. **Phantom Related Topics Resolution:** Four related topics are referenced but don't exist: `jihad-types`, `greater-lesser-jihad`, `treaty-fulfillment`, `conflict-prevention`. Should any be created? `jihad-types` and `greater-lesser-jihad` are fiqh/hadith-derived and arguably shouldn't exist as Qur'anic taxonomy topics. `treaty-fulfillment` overlaps heavily with covenant theology. `conflict-prevention` might legitimately cover deterrence (8:60) and preparedness themes.

8. **Domain Renaming Consideration:** Given that jihād transcends war/peace and covenant theology encompasses far more than conflict treaties, should the domain itself be renamed from `war-peace-conflict` to something like `striving-covenant-community-relations` or retained with explicit acknowledgment that it covers only the MARTIAL and DIPLOMATIC subsets of broader concepts?

9. **Prisoner Ethics Topic Need:** 47:4 establishes a specific prisoner-of-war framework (secure bonds → mann or fidāʾ), and 76:8 mandates feeding captives. Is this sufficient for a dedicated `prisoner-captive-ethics` topic, or should it remain under `rules-of-engagement`? The Qur'an treats captive welfare as a MORAL OBLIGATION distinct from combat rules.

10. **Bayʿah (48:10) Classification:** The pledge of allegiance at Hudaybiyyah is simultaneously a covenant (ʿahd), a political act, and a spiritual commitment. Does it belong under peace-agreements (Hudaybiyyah context), covenant theology (bayʿah = ʿahd Allāh), or a new `prophetic-allegiance` topic? Its placement affects how users encounter the concept.
