## 1. Concept Inventory

### 1.1 Jamāl (ج م ل) — Beauty

**Definition in Qur'anic usage:** Physical beauty/appearance, specifically of livestock and as an adjective for patience.

**Occurrences (exhaustive):**
- **16:6** — *jamālun* (جَمَالٌ): "And for you in them [livestock] is beauty (*jamāl*) when you bring them in and when you send them out." Form I masdar/noun. Refers to aesthetic pleasure derived from watching well-tended animals. This is the ONLY occurrence of the noun *jamāl* in the entire Qur'an.
- **12:18** — *jamīl* (جَمِيلٌ): "fa-ṣabrun jamīl" — "patience that is beautiful/fitting." Adjective modifying *ṣabr*. Jacob's response to false news about Joseph.
- **12:83** — *jamīl* (جَمِيلٌ): "fa-ṣabrun jamīl" — identical phrase, Jacob's second utterance.

**Critical finding:** The word *jamāl* occurs exactly ONCE in the Qur'an (16:6), referring to livestock aesthetics. The adjective *jamīl* occurs TWICE (12:18, 12:83), both modifying *ṣabr* (patience), not physical beauty. There is NO Qur'anic verse stating "Allah is beautiful" using the root j-m-l. The famous statement "Inna Allaha jamīlun yuḥibbu al-jamāl" is a hadith (Sahih Muslim 91a), NOT a Qur'anic text. The existing topic `husn-concept` with scope "Allah is beautiful, loves beauty" conflates hadith provenance with Qur'anic content.

**No occurrences found:** *ajmal*, *tajammul*, *istijmāl*, or any other j-m-l derivative.

### 1.5 Artistic Expression/Craftsmanship/Material Culture

**Craftsmanship verses (verified):**
- **21:80** — *ṣanʿata labūsin lakum*: Dawud taught armor-making. Divine teaching of craft for PROTECTION.
- **34:10-11** — Iron softened for Dawud; commanded to make *sābighāt* (full coats of mail) and *qaddir fī al-sard* (calculate links precisely). Craft as divine favor + ethical command (*wa-iʿmalū ṣāliḥan*).
- **34:13** — Sulayman's jinn craftsmen: *maḥārīb* (elevated chambers/sanctuaries), *tamāthīl* (statues/images), *jifān kal-jawāb* (large basins), *qudūr rāsiyāt* (stationary cauldrons). DESCRIPTIVE narrative detail. Command: *iʿmalū āla Dāwūda shukran* — work in GRATITUDE. No prohibition or endorsement of images as art category.
- **38:36-38** — Wind, builders (*bannā'*), divers (*ghawwāṣ*) subjected to Sulayman. Narrative detail.
- **18:96** — Dhul-Qarnayn iron barrier: engineering for DEFENSE.

**Negative material culture:**
- **26:128-129** — Aad building monuments (*āyah taʿbathūn*, *maṣāniʿ*) in vanity. CRITIQUE of monumentalism.
- **89:7-8** — Iram dhāt al-ʿimād: mentioned as destroyed nation, not celebrated architecture.
- **7:138, 20:88** — Golden calf (*ʿijl jasadan lahu khuwār*): idolatrous material culture, CONDEMNED.

**Poetry (*shiʿr/shuʿarā'*):**
- **26:224-227** — Poets followed by deviators; exception for believing poets who do righteous deeds. RESTRICTIVE framing.
- **36:69** — *wa-mā ʿallamnāhu al-shiʿra wa-mā yanbaghī lah*: Prophet explicitly NOT taught poetry, it is NOT befitting him. DENIAL of poetic identity.
- **21:5, 69:41** — Accusations of being a poet are REBUTTALS, not affirmations.

**Critical finding:** Craftsmanship appears ONLY in prophetic narratives as divine favors or historical details. There is NO Qur'anic discourse establishing "art" or "creativity" as independent subject categories. Poetry is explicitly DISTINGUISHED from revelation and presented negatively except when subordinated to faith. Material culture appears either as utilitarian craft (armor, construction) or as condemned vanity/idolatry. The existing topic `art-boundaries` maps 31:6 (*lahw al-ḥadīth*), 17:32, 24:19, 49:11 — NONE of which address art; they address immoral speech, sexual ethics, slander, and ridicule respectively. These are MISCLASSIFIED.

### Domain-Level Assessment

The domain "Arts, Beauty & Aesthetics" as currently constituted assumes a contemporary academic taxonomy that does NOT correspond to identifiable Qur'anic subject matter. The Qur'an addresses:
1. **Divine perfection of creation** (subordinate to signs/tawhid)
2. **Adornment as test/deception** (subordinate to ethics/eschatology)
3. **Modesty regulations** (subordinate to law)
4. **Craft as divine favor in prophetic narratives** (subordinate to prophet stories)
5. **Poetry as distinct from revelation** (subordinate to revelation/prophethood)

None of these constitute an independent "arts" domain. They are distributed across creation-signs, ethics, law, prophets, and revelation domains.

1. **Q1:** Does the single occurrence of *jamāl* (16:6, livestock beauty) establish "beauty" as an independent Qur'anic concept, or is it merely a descriptive attribute within the enumeration of livestock benefits (*dif', manāfiʿ, akhl, jamāl*)? If the latter, it should remain a subordinate detail within creation-signs or provision topics, not a standalone domain.

2. **Q2:** Can the 4-5 borderline aesthetic verses (32:7, 40:64, 64:3, 95:4, 27:88) sustain a "divine beauty/perfection" topic WITHOUT duplicating the creation-signs domain? These verses use *aḥsana* (perfected) and *atqana* (made precise), which may be better classified as divine attributes of perfection (*itqān*) rather than aesthetic beauty.

3. **Q3:** Should the existing `husn-concept` topic be REDEFINED to exclude hadith-derived content and re-scoped to only the 4-5 borderline aesthetic verses, or should it be MERGED into creation-signs as a sub-topic of divine perfection? The current scope ("Allah is beautiful, loves beauty") is demonstrably non-Qur'anic.

4. **Q4:** Are the 4 mappings under `art-boundaries` (31:6, 17:32, 24:19, 49:11) salvageable under ANY arts-related interpretation, or must they be RECLASSIFIED entirely to their actual subjects (immoral speech, sexual ethics, slander, social etiquette)?

5. **Q5:** Does the Qur'anic treatment of *zīnah* warrant a dedicated "adornment ethics" dimension, given that ~70% of occurrences are negative (deception/test) and the positive personal-adornment verses (24:31, 33:33) are legal rulings belonging to the law domain?

6. **Q6:** Is there ANY Qur'anic basis for distinguishing "Islamic art" or "creative expression" as categories, or are these entirely post-Qur'anic cultural/editorial constructs that should be explicitly labeled as such if retained?

7. **Q7:** Should the prophetic craftsmanship narratives (Dawud's armor, Sulayman's builders) be cross-referenced under a "prophetic professions/craft" topic in the work-vocation or prophets domain, rather than under arts?

### Domain-Level Recommendation

**DISSOLVE AND REDISTRIBUTE**

The domain `arts-beauty-aesthetics` should be DISSOLVED. Its constituent concepts, where textually grounded, should be redistributed as follows:

| Concept | Destination Domain | Destination Dimension/Topic |
|---------|-------------------|----------------------------|
| Divine perfection of creation (32:7, 40:64, 64:3, 95:4, 27:88) | creation-signs | New topic: "divine-perfection-itqan" or merge into existing signs topic |
| Cosmic adornment (15:16, 37:6, 41:12, 50:6, 67:5) | creation-signs | Existing signs-and-reflection topic |
| Color/diversity as signs (30:22, 35:27-28) | creation-signs | Existing diversity-divine-sign topic |
| Modesty/adornment regulations (24:31, 33:33) | law / family | Existing or new modesty topic |
| Worldly adornment as test/deception (3:14, 57:20, 18:46, etc.) | ethics / human-experiences | Existing worldliness/temptation topic |
| Sabrun jamīl (12:18, 12:83) | ethics-character | Existing patience topic |
| Iḥsān (all occurrences) | ethics-character | Existing excellence-ihsan or good-character topic |
| Prophetic craftsmanship (21:80, 34:10-13, 38:36-38) | prophets / work-vocation | Existing prophetic-professions or craftsmanship topic |
| Poetry distinction (26:224-227, 36:69) | revelation | Existing revelation-nature topic |
| Negative material culture (26:128-129, 89:7-8, 7:138, 20:88) | prophets / ethics | Existing prophetic-narratives or vanity topic |

**Rationale for dissolution:** The Qur'an does not establish "arts," "beauty," or "aesthetics" as coherent independent subject areas. What contemporary taxonomies categorize as "Islamic aesthetics" is either (a) divine attributes of perfection (belonging to tawhid/creation-signs), (b) legal regulations on adornment (belonging to law), (c) ethical warnings about worldly attraction (belonging to ethics), (d) prophetic narrative details (belonging to prophets), or (e) post-Qur'anic cultural developments (calligraphy, geometric art, architecture) that have no Qur'anic textual basis. Maintaining this domain perpetuates a category error: imposing external taxonomic expectations onto a text that organizes its subject matter differently.

**Legitimate alternative:** If scholarly review determines that the 4-5 divine-perfection verses warrant a minimal standalone topic, it should be placed under `creation-signs` as "divine-perfection-itqan" with explicit acknowledgment that this is a theological/ontological concept, not an aesthetic one in the modern sense.
